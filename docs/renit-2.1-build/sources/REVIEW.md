# Renit 2.1 — modern UI review (all iOS screens)

Reviewed 2026-11-22: 8 reviewers (visual and UX lenses × 4 groups of sections) read all 104 tiles of the Figma page `2068:14158`. Then 2 skeptics re-checked every finding against the images. None were refuted; duplicates were merged. Tiles are in `tiles/`, full sections in `sections/`.

## VISUAL lens

Only partly modern. The foundations hold up: one typeface (Plus Jakarta Sans), a restrained palette, a 24pt gutter, consistent list rows with chevrons, a reused timeline/stepper, sheets with grabbers, a consistent tab bar and unusually complete empty, error and loading states (06_01, 08_02, 13-I_07, 12-H_03). Content is also realistic. The execution falls short of 2025–26 standards in four systemic ways. First, nearly every group is a 1px bordered card, often nested three deep (b3-02). Second, the bottom action bar comes in at least seven arrangements, and the primary sometimes sits inside content (b3-01). Third, colour carries no stable meaning: purple marks containers, warnings and success; destructive actions are drawn five ways, including a purple Archive confirm (b4-01, b4-04); and text on brand tint measures 4.32:1, failing AA (b1-04). Fourth, icons repeat or contradict each other (b1-01, b4-07). Hierarchy also suffers from the compressed 20/18/16 scale, with no display size for prices, OTPs or titles (b1-05), and from 7-line policy banners that cause banner blindness (b3-06, b2-03). Dark mode is offered in the Appearance sheet (13-I_06) but has no tokens or frames. Of the 53 findings, 32 are unique once 21 duplicates are merged. I sampled tiles beyond the cited ones, including 02-A-auth_04, 05-D-checkout_03, 06-E1_03/_07, 07-E2_02/_03/_05/_11, 08-F1_06, 09-F2_04, 10-G1_08, 11-G2_05, 12-H_02/_03/_10/_11, 13-I_02/_04/_05/_06/_07 and 14-J_04/_05. That sampling added the disabled-versus-tonal lavender clash, placeholder imagery in shop flows and inconsistent sheet headers. Fixing tokens (on-tint text, neutral disabled, semantic surfaces), defining three bar variants and stripping nested borders would move this from 'tidy legacy' to 'modern' without changing the look.

### High

**visual-b1-01 · Iconography** (systemic)
- Where: 03-B-discover_01 'Fitness & Sports' (both frames); 03-B-discover_05 Filters sheet, Category tab ('Fitness & Sports' list); 03-B-discover_06 Filters sheet, Condition tab; 01-samples_01 PDP '1984 – George Orwell' (Excellent / Condition); 03-B-discover_01 'All categories'; 01-samples_01 Home 'Categories'
- Problem: Icons repeat or carry no meaning. All 8 sub-categories (Tennis, Badminton, Cricket, Football, Gym, Cycle, Boxing, Board games) use the same trophy icon, both in the tile grid and in the Filters category list. Fair/Good/Excellent condition all use a lightbulb. The category grids also mix product photo cut-outs (drone, guitar, sneakers, JCB, mannequin) with tiny 20px line icons (camera, briefcase, sofa) in identical tiles, so tiles look half-finished and have different visual weight.
- Why: When every tile shows the same glyph, the icon adds no information and users must read every label, which defeats the point of an icon grid. Mixing photos and glyphs reads as unfinished, a pattern top marketplaces (Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, Airbnb category bar) avoid by using one consistent treatment.
- Fix: Pick one treatment per grid. Either use all photographic cut-outs on a neutral tile, or all line icons from a set with distinct glyphs (heroicons lacks sport icons, so add a small custom set or use photos). For sub-categories without a distinct icon, drop the icon and use text chips. Replace the condition lightbulbs with a graded visual (1–3 filled dots or a sparkle/badge scale) or no icon.
- Verifier: Re-checked by zooming into 03-B-discover_05: in the Filters Category tab, Tennis, Badminton, Cricket, Football, Gym, Cycle and Boxing all use the same trophy glyph. 10-G1_05 and 14-J_04 also use a trophy for Fitness & sports. The point that the icon carries no information stands. This is the 'same glyph repeated' half of the iconography problem; visual-b4-07 covers the 'same concept, different glyph' half.

**visual-b1-02 · Imagery & content-first** (systemic)
- Where: 00-kit_01 'Listing card' (heart spec); 03-B-discover_01 'Fitness & Sports' and 03-B-discover_04 'Fitness & Sports' results (Boxing Bag card); 03-B-discover_06 'Saved'; 01-samples_01 Home 'Top experiences'
- Problem: The save heart is a thin 1.5px black outline placed straight on the photo with no scrim, halo or backing chip. On the dark Boxing Bag photo it disappears entirely: no heart is visible on that card in either results frame, while the other cards show one. On 'Saved', the filled #635BE8 heart sits on blue photos (PS5 FIFA banner, Wilson racket on blue court) with very low contrast.

- Fix: Make the heart white (unsaved) with a 1px black/30% stroke or drop shadow, inside a 32px circle of black/25% blur. For the saved state use a white circle with a purple or red filled heart. Update the Listing card component once and it propagates.
- Verifier: Zoomed into 03-B-discover_04 (Boxing Bag card): no heart is visible on the dark photo. In 03-B-discover_06 'Saved', the purple filled heart over the PS5/FIFA banner almost disappears. On the VR cut-out the same heart reads fine on white, so the failure depends on the image. A fix in the Listing card component would propagate.

**visual-b2-03 · Density / progressive disclosure** (systemic)
- Where: 05-D-checkout_01 'Review and book' (frames 2–3); 05-D-checkout_02 'Review and book' (2 items) and 'Check your ID' ('How we use your information'); 06-E1-rentals_06 'Your Coffee machine arrives today' ('When it arrives' card)
- Problem: The booking screen carries about 8 paragraphs of 14px grey policy text between the price breakdown and the Book button. The text covers the 10-minute ring, lapse rules, the 24-hour cancellation and late days. It is preceded by a bold run-on sentence that repeats the numbers already in the table ('₹99 now, ₹1,551 after the owner accepts, ₹3,000 deposit... In all, deposit included: ₹4,650.'). 'Check your ID' shows roughly 350 words of legal copy inside a card. The delivery screen has a 5-bullet text card.
- Why: At the moment of commitment, a wall of uniform grey text has no hierarchy, so the rules users must know (lapse, non-refundable ₹99) are as easy to miss as the trivia. Airbnb, Uber and Swiggy show 2–3 key-fact rows with icons and link to the full policy.
- Fix: Replace the paragraphs with 3–4 icon rows of short bold facts ('₹99 back if they say no', 'Pay within 30 min of their yes', 'Free cancel until 24h before'). Move the full text behind a 'Cancellation and late rules' row that opens a sheet. Drop the bold summary sentence; the 'What you pay, and when' table already says it. Collapse the KYC 'How we use your information' card into an expandable row.
- Verifier: 06_06 'When it arrives' is a 5-bullet, roughly 14-line card above the CTA. 07-E2_07/_08 carry 7-line policy banners. The 05-D booking and KYC walls were seen earlier with nothing found to refute them. Uniform grey paragraphs at the moment of commitment are a real hierarchy failure.

**visual-b3-01 · Component consistency: bottom action bar** (systemic)
- Where: 07-E2-rental-end_01 'The shop isn't here' / 'The shop did not come'; 07-E2-rental-end_03 'Recorded and uploaded'; 07-E2-rental-end_04 'End the rental early?'; 07-E2-rental-end_08 'Report a fault' / 'Report a problem'; 07-E2-rental-end_10 'Claim · Damage' (reply); 08-F1-shop-today_04 'New booking' (Can't do it / Accept); 08-F1-shop-today_04 'Extend until' dialog; 08-F1-shop-today_05 'New booking · 3 items' / 'You accepted this booking'; 09-F2-shop-handover_02 'Kabir did not come'; 09-F2-shop-handover_05 'Not as described'
- Problem: The spec calls for one 52px full-width primary in a sticky bar. The bottom of the screen actually comes in at least seven arrangements: (a) a 50/50 split of an outline 'Go back' and a filled primary (07_01, 07_08, 09_02); (b) a centred text link stacked ABOVE the primary, such as 'Keep renting', 'Cancel', 'Not now', 'Refuse only' or 'Done' (07_04, 07_09, 08_05, 08_06, 08_07); (c) the primary with the escape link BELOW it ('Accept 2 of 3' then 'Can't do any', 08_05); (d) an outline negative beside a filled positive ('Can't do it' | 'Accept', 08_04); (e) the primary inside a card mid-scroll and only an outline 'Open the booking' at the bottom ('Send reply', 07_10 and 09_05; 'Confirm their set' inside the card while the sticky bar holds an outline 'Back', 07_03); (f) a centred modal dialog with 'Cancel' | 'Confirm' side by side ('Extend until', 08_04), where everything else uses bottom sheets; (g) 'Done' text plus a 'See booking' primary, so two exits read as the same action (08_05 'You accepted this booking').
- Why: Thumb memory and scan order break from screen to screen: the main action moves between the top of the bar, the bottom, the right half and inside the content. Airbnb, Uber and iOS keep one stable bottom anchor with a consistent secondary position. Here the eye has to find the action again on every screen, and on 07_03 the visually loudest button (in the card) and the anchored button (Back) contradict each other.
- Fix: Define three bar variants in the kit and use only those: (1) a single full-width primary; (2) a full-width primary with a tertiary text button BELOW it, matching the iOS sheet and Airbnb checkout order; (3) for binary decisions, outline secondary left and filled primary right at equal width. Move in-card primaries ('Send reply', 'Confirm their set') into the sticky bar while that task is active. Replace the 'Extend until' centred dialog with a sheet so it matches the other sheets.
- Verifier: Kept as the canonical bottom-bar finding. Verified variants: 07_01 has a 50/50 'Go back' plus primary; 07_04 and 07_09 put a text link above the primary; 08_05 has 'Accept 2 of 3' with 'Can't do any' below it; 08_04 pairs 'Can't do it' outline with 'Accept'; 08_04 'Extend until' is a centred dialog; 07_10 and 09_05 put 'Send reply' inside a card with only an outline 'Open the booking' at the bottom; 08_05 shows 'Done' text above 'See booking'.

**visual-b3-02 · Containers and visual noise (boxes in boxes)** (systemic)
- Where: 09-F2-shop-handover_01 'Hand over the DJI Mini 4 Pro' and 'Deliver the Epson projector'; 07-E2-rental-end_03 'Recorded and uploaded' ('Ask the shop for the code' card); 07-E2-rental-end_05 'Return the Bluetooth speaker'; 07-E2-rental-end_10 'Claim · Damage' ('Reply to this claim'); 08-F1-shop-today_07 'Booking RN-24831 Ready' ('Customer' card); 09-F2-shop-handover_05 'Not as described'
- Problem: Almost every content group is a 1px bordered rounded card, and cards routinely hold more bordered containers. 'Hand over the DJI Mini 4 Pro' stacks about seven bordered cards. Inside them sit the two bordered 'Yours' / 'Kabir's' cards holding grey photo tiles, the 'Let Kabir know' card holding outlined chips and an outlined 'Call Kabir' button, and the 'Type Kabir's code' card holding six bordered digit boxes, so there are three levels of outline. The same nesting appears in 'Reply to this claim' (card > bordered text field > dashed add-photo box > button), the 'Customer' card holding a tinted info banner (08_07), and the 'Return the Bluetooth speaker' stepper card holding six OTP boxes (07_05).
- Why: When everything is boxed, the boxes stop showing grouping and become texture. The screen reads as a form rather than a task, and hierarchy flattens. Current Airbnb trip pages, Uber trip detail and iOS 18 Settings/Wallet group content with whitespace, section headers and full-bleed dividers, and keep containers for elevated or interactive items.
- Fix: Make 'section header + content on the page background, separated by 24-32px' the default. Keep a card only for an actionable or summary object (the item row, the money summary). Never put a bordered element inside a bordered card: inside a card, use dividers or a subtle tinted fill. Specifically, remove the outer borders on 'Yours'/'Kabir's' and use one comparison grid with column labels, drop the card around the stepper + OTP, and render 'Reply to this claim' as a page section.
- Verifier: Kept as the canonical boxes-in-boxes finding. 09_01 'Hand over the DJI Mini 4 Pro' stacks about 7 bordered cards, holding bordered 'Yours/Kabir's' sub-cards with grey tiles, outlined chips plus an outlined button, and bordered OTP cells. The same pattern runs through 06_05, 07_05, 07_10, 08_07 (info banner inside the 'Customer' card), 12-H_06 'Delivery' and 14-J_01 FAQs. It is the most pervasive visual issue.

**visual-b4-01 · Component consistency (destructive actions)** (systemic)
- Where: 11-G2-products_02 'Archive product' sheet; 11-G2-products_01/02 'Edit product'; 12-H-shop-settings_07 'Discard changes?'; 13-I-account_06 'Are you sure you want to delete your account?'; 13-I-account_07 'Delete this address?'; 13-I-account_03 'Personal details'; 13-I-account_08 'Business billing'
- Problem: Destructive confirmations are drawn at least five ways. 'Archive product' confirms with a FILLED PURPLE primary button, which is the brand CTA colour. 'Discard' is a red-outlined full-width button above a 'Cancel' link. 'Delete' (account) is a red-tinted filled half-width button beside an outline Cancel, inside a centred alert. 'Delete address' is a red-tinted full-width button below a 'Cancel' link. The entry points also differ: 'Archive' is a red-text outline button, 'Delete my account' and 'Remove' are red text links. Only the text links match the spec's 'destructive red text'.
- Why: Users learn colour as meaning. A purple confirm on an archive action looks safe and positive, so people tap without thinking. iOS HIG and Material 3 both use one destructive style everywhere (red label, and an action sheet or alert with the destructive role) so it reads instantly.
- Fix: Define one destructive confirm component as a sheet: title, consequence text, a full-width red-text (or red-tinted) destructive button, and a Cancel text button below it. Use it for Archive, Discard, Delete address, Delete account and Remove GSTIN. Never put a destructive confirm on the purple fill.
- Verifier: Kept as the canonical destructive-action finding. 11-G2_02 'Archive product' confirms with a filled purple button. 12-H_07 puts a red-outlined 'Discard' above a 'Cancel' link, while 07_09 'Discard changes?' puts 'Cancel' above a grey-bordered red 'Discard', so the same sheet is drawn two ways. 13-I_06 is a centred alert with a half-width pale-red 'Delete'. 13-I_07 'Delete address' is pale-red, full-width and below Cancel. 08_04 and 08_08 use pale-red fills.

### Medium

**visual-b1-04 · Colour discipline & contrast** (systemic)
- Where: 00-kit_01 'Status pill' (Confirmed); 00-kit_02 'Chip' (selected 'Good'); 00-kit_03 'Empty state' ('Browse items'); 03-B-discover_01 'Couldn't load categories' (Retry); 03-B-discover_04 'No matches' (Clear filters) and Filters sheet ('Wilson 4' selected chip); 03-B-discover_03 'Tennis' (date chip 'Nov 28 – Nov 30', 'Change' link on grey banner); 03-B-discover_07 'Nothing saved yet' (Browse listings)
- Problem: The brand-on-tint pairing fails WCAG AA. Measured #635BE8 text on the lavender tint (237,237,252) is 4.32:1, and the 'Change' link on the grey delivery banner (242,242,242) is 4.47:1. Both are below 4.5:1 for 12–16px text, and none of these are 'large text' (≥18.66 bold). The pairing is used for every selected chip, every tonal button, the brand status pill and the filter tabs' selected chips.
- Why: Selected states and tonal actions are where users confirm what they picked. A systemic token-level AA failure will be flagged by accessibility audits, and it will get worse in sunlight on Indian outdoor use.
- Fix: Add an 'on-brand-tint' text token darker than the CTA purple (around #4E46D4, aiming for ≥4.5:1 on #EDEDFC; verify with a checker) and use it for text on lavender or grey surfaces. Keep #635BE8 for filled buttons (white on it passes) and for links on pure white (5.01:1).
- Verifier: Kept as the canonical contrast finding. Sampled from the pixels of a selected chip in 07-E2-rental-end_07: fill (237,237,252) and text (99,91,232) = #635BE8, which works out to 4.32:1 for 12–14px text, so it fails AA. Purple on white is 5.01:1 and on #F2F2F2 is 4.47:1. A darker on-tint token (e.g. #4E46D4 = 5.75:1) fixes every instance at once.

**visual-b1-05 · Visual hierarchy** (systemic)
- Where: 00-kit_01 'Top bar' and 00-kit_02 'Section header' (both 18 Bold); 01-samples_01 Home, PDP '1984 – George Orwell'; 02-A-auth_07 'Saved', 'My Profile'; 03-B-discover_07 'About the owner'
- Problem: The 20/18/16/14/12 scale is too compressed to produce a focal point. The screen title, section headers, sheet titles and empty-state titles are all 18–20 Bold. On the PDP, the product title (18), 'Specifications' (18) and the price '₹19' (16 Bold) read at nearly the same weight, so neither the title nor the price leads. Tab-root screens ('Saved', 'My Profile') have an 18–20 title that matches the body headings below, and Home has no title or location header, only a search pill and equal-weight rails.
- Why: Modern consumer apps use a clear display step for screen titles and key numbers: iOS large titles at 34, Airbnb's 26–32 PDP titles, Swiggy and Zomato's bold location/greeting header. Without it, every screen reads as a flat list and the price, the decision driver in a rental marketplace, does not stand out.
- Fix: Add one or two display steps (24 and 28/32 Bold) for tab-root large titles, the PDP title and the PDP/checkout price. Keep 18 for section headers and leave the rest of the scale. On Home, add a location/greeting header above search so the screen has a clear entry point.
- Verifier: Kept over b3-08. In the PDP crop (04-C), the H1 '1984 – George Orwell' (~18) and the price '₹19/day' (~16 bold) sit at almost the same weight as the 18 nav title above them. On 07-E2_04/_05 'Rent due', the ₹1,800 hero is ~20, barely above the 18 nav title. Tab roots ('Rentals' 06_01, 'Profile' 12-H_01) use 18–20, the same as section headers. A display step is missing.

**visual-b1-11 · Dark-mode readiness** (systemic)
- Where: Spec (text = black at 100/70/60%); 00-kit_02 'Notice card' and 'Status pill'; 00-kit_02 'Chip'; all frames in 00–03 are light-only
- Problem: This is a spec-level risk, not an observed defect. Text is defined as black at fixed opacities, and surfaces use fixed light pastels (measured: lavender 237,237,252; warning 255,250,235; success 236,253,243; banner grey 242,242,242). Black-alpha text does not invert, and fixed pastels cannot be reused on dark surfaces. No dark-mode frames exist to validate.
- Why: iOS users expect dark mode by default in 2025-26 apps (Apple's own apps, Uber, Airbnb), and retrofitting semantic tokens after build is costly.
- Fix: Define semantic tokens now (text-primary/secondary/tertiary, surface, surface-tint-brand/success/warning/danger, border, on-brand-tint) with light and dark values in Figma variable modes. Mock 3–4 key screens (Home, PDP, Filters sheet, Rentals) in dark mode to catch tint and contrast issues.
- Verifier: Kept as the canonical dark-mode finding. 13-I-account_06 shows an 'Appearance' sheet offering Dark mode, yet no dark frames or dark token values exist anywhere, so this is a real gap and not speculation. b2-16 is wrong to say black-at-opacity 'inverts cleanly': it does not. Text tokens need semantic light and dark values.

**visual-b2-04 · Visual hierarchy: one primary per screen, in one place** (systemic)

- Problem: The sticky-bottom-bar rule is broken on several key screens. In 'Your cart' the checkout actions are compact buttons inside the cards (filled on the single-shop cart, outlined on the multi-shop cart) and the only filled button is in a banner at the top. ## UX lens

For UX and interaction, Renit 2.0 partly meets modern principles. Its strengths are real and above average for the category. Money is shown up front and in stages (the 'What you pay, and when' card in 05-D-checkout_01). The camera-only condition recording is well guided, with step strip, framing overlay, and 'what is recorded / who sees it / how long' (07-E2-rental-end_02). Errors give specific recovery steps (05-D-checkout_03 PAN error, 05-D-checkout_04 KYC states). The Delivery settings screen shows an 'Unsaved: … Discard' summary (12-H-shop-settings_05). Most CTAs use plain verb+noun wording. It falls short in five systemic ways:
- Policy and consequences are written as walls of prose instead of scannable rows (b2-01).
- Dates and totals arrive late in the funnel (b2-02, b1-05).
- The one-sticky-primary rule is broken in key places: the cart, ID-gated review, split 'Go back' bars and mid-screen confirms (b2-03, b3-02, b3-06).
- Destructive and blocked actions are handled inconsistently (b4-01, b4-02, b2-09).
- Empty, error and disabled states often give no action or reason (b4-03 and the missed item).
Platform conventions also lag: a Material back arrow on iOS, dialogs mixed with sheets, custom DOB and time pickers. In auth, phone-first sign-in, the expected default for India, is buried (b1-01). Of the 59 findings, 39 were confirmed, 13 partly confirmed (mostly overstated measurements or claims contradicted by other frames), 7 were duplicates, and none were refuted.

**ux-b1-01 · Forms and input / modern expected patterns (India)** (systemic)
- Where: 02-A-auth_01 (Rent anything / Rent anywhere / Rent anytime landing), 02-A-auth_01-_02 'Welcome to Renit' and 'Continue with your mobile number', 02-A-auth_02-_03 'Enter verification code'
- Problem: Phone sign-in is hidden. The landing page offers Google, Apple and email; phone OTP is only reachable through 'Continue with email' and then a text link, 'Use my phone number instead'. **ux-b1-06 · System states (loading, offline)** (systemic, partly)

- Problem: Within sections 00–03, loading is shown only as a centred spinner on a blank screen (02-A-auth_06, both the location fetch and the T&C page).
